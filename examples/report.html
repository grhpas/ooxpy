Tutorial 4: Structured Word reports

Sections, headers and footers, links, notes, comments, a table of contents and template placeholders. Script: examples/report.py

Headers, footers and page numbers

A document is made of sections; each has its own page setup and headers. {PAGE}, {NUMPAGES} and {SECTIONPAGES} become fields that Word fills in. A tab (\t) moves to the center, a second one to the right margin:

from oox import WordprocessingDocument
from oox.ns import w

doc = WordprocessingDocument.new()
doc.add_heading("Report", 0)
doc.add_paragraph("Body text.")
sec = doc.sections[0]
sec.header_text("Example Ltd\t\tConfidential")
sec.footer_text("Page {PAGE} of {NUMPAGES}", align="center")
sec.different_first_page = True            # nothing on page 1 ...
sec.header_text("Cover", kind="first")     # ... unless you add a "first" header
print(sec)
<Section portrait 595.3x841.9 pt>

Kinds are "default", "first" and "even" (an "even" header switches on different odd and even pages).

A landscape section

wide = doc.add_section(landscape=True)     # starts on a new page
doc.add_heading("Appendix", 1)
print([s.orientation for s in doc.sections], wide.linked_to_previous("footer"))
wide.header_text("Appendix", align="right")  # its own header; the footer stays linked
['portrait', 'landscape'] True

A new section shows the previous section's headers and footers ("Link to Previous" in Word) until you give it its own.

Links, bookmarks and notes

p = doc.add_paragraph("See ")
doc.add_hyperlink(p, "Ecma", target="https://ecma-international.org/")
p.add(w.R(w.T(" and ", space="preserve")))
doc.add_hyperlink(p, "the appendix", anchor="appendix")        # internal link
doc.add_bookmark("appendix", doc.add_heading("Figures", 2))
doc.add_footnote(p, "A footnote.")
doc.add_endnote(p, "An endnote.")
doc.add_comment(p, "Please check.", author="Ada Lovelace")
print([(link.text, link.target or link.anchor) for link in doc.hyperlinks()])
print([note.text for note in doc.footnotes()], doc.comments()[0].author)
[('Ecma', 'https://ecma-international.org/'), ('the appendix', 'appendix')]
['A footnote.'] Ada Lovelace

The readers work on documents from Word and LibreOffice too. Styles such as Hyperlink or Footnote Text are added the first time they are needed.

Table of contents

from oox import teletype

toc = doc.add_toc(levels=(1, 2), before=doc.body[1])   # after the title
print([teletype.extractText(p) for p in toc])
['Contents', 'Appendix', 'Figures']

The TOC is a real Word field, pre-filled with the current headings. Word adds the page numbers when the field is updated (right-click > Update Field, or F9). update_on_open=True makes Word offer the update when the file opens, but that shows a prompt.

Templates: fill in placeholders

Word splits text into runs wherever formatting or editing history changes, so {{name}} is often stored in several pieces. replace_text matches across runs, in the body, headers, footers, notes and comments:

doc.body.add(w.P(w.R(w.T("Dear {{na")), w.R(w.RPr(w.B()), w.T("me}},"))))
print(doc.replace_text({"{{name}}": "Ada"}))
doc.save("report.docx")
{'{{name}}': 1}

The replacement takes the formatting of the run where the placeholder starts. open_document("template.docx") then replace_text then save is the whole template workflow.
