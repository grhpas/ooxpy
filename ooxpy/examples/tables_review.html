Tutorial 5: Tables, floating pictures and tracked changes

Merged cells, header rows, pictures with text wrapping, and suggested edits you can accept or reject. Script: examples/tables_review.py

Tables

add_table takes rows of values. Header rows repeat on every page and are bold; widths (from oox.units, or twips) fix the column widths:

from oox import WordprocessingDocument
from oox.units import Cm

doc = WordprocessingDocument.new()
t = doc.add_table([["Region", "Product", "Q1"],
                   ["North", "Widgets", 120],
                   [None, "Gadgets", 80],
                   ["Total", None, 200]],
                  header_rows=1, widths=[Cm(4), Cm(4), Cm(3)])
t.merge(1, 0, 2, 0)                       # "North" covers two rows
t.merge(3, 0, 3, 1)                       # "Total" spans two columns
t.format_cell(0, 0, fill="D9E2F3", align="center")
t.format_cell(3, 0, align="right", bold=True)
print(t.values())
[['Region', 'Product', 'Q1'], ['North', 'Widgets', '120'], [None, 'Gadgets', '80'], ['Total', None, '200']]

Coordinates are grid positions, counted from 0, and they don't shift after a merge: t.cell(2, 0) is the merged "North" cell. values() shows None where a merge covers a position. doc.tables() reads the tables of any document, including ones made in Word.

Floating pictures

title = doc.add_heading("Sales", 0)
doc.add_floating_picture("chart.png", width=Cm(3), h_align="right",
                         h_relative="margin", paragraph=title, wrap="square")
doc.add_floating_picture("chart.png", width=Cm(12), h_align="center", v_align="center",
                         h_relative="page", v_relative="page", wrap="behind")
print([p["wrap"] for p in doc.pictures()])
['square', 'behind']

A floating picture is anchored to a paragraph and positioned relative to the page, margin, column or paragraph, by alignment or by offset (x=, y=). Wrapping: square, tight, through, topAndBottom, front and behind.

Suggest edits (tracked changes)

p = doc.add_paragraph("Sales grew by 8% in 2025.")
doc.suggest_replace(p, "8%", "9%", author="Finance")
doc.suggest_insert(p, " Unaudited.", author="Finance")
doc.track_changes = True                  # Word keeps tracking later edits
print([(r["kind"], r["text"]) for r in doc.revisions()])
[('delete', '8%'), ('insert', '9%'), ('insert', ' Unaudited.')]

Word shows these as suggestions to accept or reject. Matching works across runs, as in replace_text.

Accept or reject

from oox import teletype

print(doc.accept_all(), teletype.extractText(p))
doc.save("sales.docx")
3 Sales grew by 9% in 2025. Unaudited.

accept_all() and reject_all() also handle what Word writes: moves, inserted or deleted paragraphs and table rows, and formatting changes, in the body, headers, footers, notes and comments. Use them to produce a clean copy of a reviewed document.
